Boise Resale Housing Market 2026: Fewer Listings, More Equity With a $3,777 Flat Fee

By Jeff Jones, Designated Broker & Owner, 208 Premier Real Estate

Boise, Meridian and Eagle homeowners considering a flat fee MLS listing have a compelling reason to assess a winter sale: existing-home competition is below last year's levels, while closed sales remain active. Here is what that means for pricing, marketing and your net proceeds.

October 2026 existing/resale market: 892 active listings (−15.0% year over year), 512 closed resale sales (−3.2%), a $556,000 median resale sales price (+3.9%), and just 1.7 months of resale inventory versus 2.2 months one year earlier.

The resale market, not new construction

All figures in this comparison describe existing/resale single-family homes in Ada County. They are not city-only figures for Boise, Meridian or Eagle, and they exclude new construction. Each market segment and price range behaves differently; a property-specific comparative market analysis is essential.

Source: Boise Regional REALTORS® / Intermountain MLS, July–October monthly market stat sheets and the October 2026 Existing/Resale report dated November 10, 2026. October 2025 figures use the updated comparison values in the October 2026 report.

How resale prices have changed

In 2026, the resale median climbed to $600,000 in July, then eased to $579,900 in August, $561,065 in September and $556,000 in October. That is a 7.3% decline from July's median; however, October remained 3.9% above the revised October 2025 median of $535,000. The fall retreat does not by itself mean individual properties lost 7.3% of their value: changes in which homes sold affect the median. In particular, the age, location and price mix of resales shifts month to month.

At the same time, inventory has tightened steadily: 1,067 active resales in July 2026, 991 in August, 955 in September and 892 in October. October's 892 homes were 157 fewer than the 1,049 reported for October 2025. That is meaningful competition reduction even though buyers still compare condition, location, financing costs and asking price.

Why November and December may favor prepared sellers

The previous winter illustrates the opportunity. From October to December 2025, Ada County's available resale inventory fell from 1,049 to 614 homes (−41.5%), while monthly closed resale sales fell from 529 to 405 (−23.4%, using the revised October comparison). Even in December, hundreds of resale homes closed. The pool of competing listings contracted much faster than the monthly volume of completed sales.

That does not prove any one home will sell more quickly in winter. Inventory is a month-end snapshot; closings reflect contracts that may have been signed weeks earlier, and seasonal changes involve both buyers and sellers. But it disproves the assumption that winter means no buyers. With fewer comparable resale homes available, a well-presented, accurately priced listing can receive attention from serious buyers who still need to move.

What the data means for your asking price

  • Price against current competition. Evaluate recently closed comparable resales, active listings and properties under contract—not the countywide median alone.
  • Watch showing activity. The October 2026 report puts existing-home days on market at 31, compared with 35 in its October 2025 comparison. The right target is your home's segment, not the county average.
  • Present the home for winter. Excellent photographs, flexible showing access and a clear description of the home's practical features matter when daylight is limited.
  • Compare net proceeds. An extra month of carrying costs and price concessions can outweigh an anticipated seasonal premium. Consider both winter and spring plans on estimated net proceeds.
