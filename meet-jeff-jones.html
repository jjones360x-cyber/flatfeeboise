Not just an agent: what “Designated Broker” means in Idaho

In Idaho, a Designated Broker is the licensed professional responsible for supervising the brokerage and its licensees, enforcing applicable real estate requirements, and maintaining the firm's compliance. It is a distinct license role with greater responsibility than a salesperson—not a claim that the government ranks individual REALTORS® by prestige.

An actively licensed Idaho broker can also prepare a formal Broker Price Opinion (BPO), subject to Idaho's written-report and disclosure requirements, and may charge for that service. Salespersons can prepare ordinary comparative market analyses for prospective listings and sales but cannot independently issue formal BPOs. A BPO is not an appraisal. I bring that pricing discipline to my listing consultations, along with local comparable sales, active competition and current market conditions.

Regulatory references: Idaho Code § 54-2038 and the Idaho Real Estate License Law and Rules — BPO Guidance. A designated broker is a real estate license classification, while REALTOR® is a membership designation.

$200 communication guarantee

If I fail to return a seller's call, text or email within 24 hours—or fail to provide the agreed weekly update—I will credit $200 against my listing fee at closing per qualifying occurrence, subject to the written guarantee and its documentation and eligibility terms.

02

Love the home—or I'll help sell it for free

For a qualifying buyer I represent, if the buyer does not love the home purchased, I will waive my listing-side brokerage fee to help resell it within one year of purchase, subject to written conditions. It does not eliminate other sale expenses, third-party fees or any separately negotiated buyer-broker fee.

03

30-day qualified cancellation guarantee

Under the qualifying written guarantee, once 30 days have passed, a seller who has agreed with me on the listing price may cancel the listing without my early-termination fee, subject to the agreement's stated conditions. The signed agreement controls; otherwise early-cancellation or reimbursement terms may apply.

Guarantees are not unconditional. Eligibility, timing, notification, applicable fees and other restrictions must be set out in the signed agreement or written addendum before a consumer relies on the offer. The $200 communication credit is applied against the listing fee payable at closing, not necessarily a cash payment independent of closing.
